Beacon, on a tab shell

Beacon’s dark, emissive, high-vis idea, carried on Workbench’s navigation: a bottom tab bar with sub-tabs inside a tab. Dark is the default; the palette has moved from warm graphite to a cool near-black so the signal reads as a light source rather than a paper accent, and the signal itself is a hotter, more fluorescent amber than Field Manual’s.

The decision this is built from

Beacon’s idea, Workbench’s structure, style still open. Recorded from the owner: the dark high-visibility direction is liked, the bottom tab navigation and the sub-tabs are wanted, and the visual style is acknowledged as unfinished.

This file is the structural answer plus one pass at the style. The open questions at the bottom are the parts I do not think are settled yet, and they are the things to react to.

Everywhere else — the call dock returns

On any other screen a red Call 999 dock sits directly above the tab bar, so the emergency action is never more than one tap away and never off-screen.

Call 999
ActLocateFieldMore

Why one rule and not two stacked bars everywhere. A dock plus a tab bar on Act would show two red things and spend 100px of the thumb zone restating an action already on screen. Tying the dock to “anywhere that isn’t Act” keeps Beacon’s promise — the emergency action is always reachable — while keeping exactly one red thing on every screen. The cost, honestly: on non-Act tabs the bottom chrome is about 106px plus the safe area. If that is too much, the alternative is a red fifth item in the tab bar itself, which makes the emergency action a peer of “More” — worse, in my view, but it is a real option.

Sub-tabs, not more screens. Locate holds Map · Where I am · Compass, and Field holds Capture · Report. CPR and the defibrillator list are pushed from Act and keep Act lit in the tab bar, because they are Act territory rather than places of their own.

What I think still needs work

These are the style calls I would not make on your behalf. React to them and the next pass can settle them.

The signal colour
I moved it to a hotter, more fluorescent amber (hue 72, chroma 0.19) on a cool near-black, so it reads as light emitted rather than a warm paper accent. That is what separates it from Field Manual, which was otherwise the same palette at night. Is fluorescent right, or does it read as hazard-tape?
CURRENT FIELD MANUAL GREENER
The voice of the type
Everything is still the system font, which is honest for a product UI but gives the app no character of its own. The voice currently comes only from scale, weight and the mono numerals. A condensed grotesque would suit a field instrument; it also means bundling a font, which the app has never done. Worth deciding before more styling, because it changes every layout.
How much bottom chrome is too much
On non-Act tabs the dock plus tab bar is about 106px plus the safe area — roughly a sixth of the screen. It buys the guarantee that Call 999 is never lost. The alternative is a red fifth tab, which demotes the emergency action to a peer of “More”. I think the dock is right, but it is the call I am least sure of.
Dark-first, honestly
Dark is the default and light is the variant. That is defensible for a low-light emergency tool, but it is a statement about when this app is used. It also makes the light theme the one that gets less attention, which is a real risk for outdoor daytime use — arguably the most common case.

What this file is and is not. It is a structural answer and one style pass, not a final design — the four cards above are the parts I consider unsettled. The map is drawn in CSS as a stand-in for MapLibre. Every clinical string is the real cited record or an honest content slot. Dark is the default; the second Act frame is forced light so both can be seen at once. Nothing here has been applied to the app — the plan for that is docs/plans/beacon-tab-navigation.md.
