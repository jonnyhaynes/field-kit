Field Kit · design direction 3 of 3

Workbench

A field instrument with compartments. The app has places rather than a stack of pages: a tab bar you can move around in, a map-first Locate surface, and a Field compartment that only appears when the Responder depth is on. Cool graphite and a surveying lime, because this is navigation and measurement equipment, not a hospital.

The structural bet is the tab bar. Directions 1 and 2 keep the app as one root screen with everything pushed on top of it. This one gives the app a floor plan: Act is the default and the loudest tab, but a responder can live in Locate while working, and the depth adds a compartment instead of a link.

The bet
Make the app navigable. The AED map and the capture forms deserve somewhere to live, not a back button.
Structure
Four tabs: Act · Locate · Field · More. Field appears with the Responder depth.
Colour discipline
Surveying lime for the tooling, red once for the emergency. No clinical blue, no teal.

Depth is still not a mode, and the tab bar makes that literal. Guided users get three compartments; turning the Responder depth on adds a fourth. Act is untouched either way, and it is the tab the app opens on. This is the one direction where the structure itself makes the depth visible — which is either its best idea or its worst, depending on how much the tab bar dilutes the single dominant action.

What it gains, what it costs

Gains

  • The AED map finally has a home. Finding a defibrillator while walking is a first-class job here, not a push off a list.
  • The Responder depth is visible in the structure, which makes “adds, never moves” something a user can actually see.
  • It scales. Guidance, packs, notes and settings have compartments, so the app can grow without Act growing with it.

Costs

  • The tab bar competes with the emergency action. This is the real risk: four equally-weighted destinations at the bottom of the screen is exactly the “too many equal calls to action” the brief warns about.
  • Call 999 is only on one tab. The other directions can promise it is always on screen; this one cannot, unless a dock is added on top of the tabs — two bars, which is worse.
  • A map-first Locate needs position to be useful. Indoors or with location off, the signature screen is a blank map, so it needs a strong no-fix state to not feel broken.

How to read this file. Light is the default; flip your OS appearance to see the dark variant, and one frame is forced dark. The map is drawn in CSS — it is a stand-in for MapLibre, present so the composition can be judged, not a proposal for how the map should be rendered.
