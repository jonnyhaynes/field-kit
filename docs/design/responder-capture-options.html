Field Kit · responder capture

The capture form is 47 fields on one screen

Looked at on the simulator rather than imagined. The Field tab renders all four mnemonics as cards in a single scroll: SAMPLER 8 fields, ABCDE 21, ETHANE 8, ASHICE 10. That is the whole complaint, measured — and it is worse than "a lot", because the four are not alternatives to each other in any way the screen admits.

Everything below is the same palette and type as the app, so these frames can be compared with what is actually on the phone.

Half way down. ABCDE alone is taller than two screens. A responder who wants ASHICE scrolls past all of it, and a responder who is in ABCDE cannot see how much of it is left.

There is nowhere to be
Four mnemonics, one scroll, no index and no position indicator. You cannot tell whether you are at the start, the middle, or one card from the end — and ABCDE alone is 45% of the form.
The four are not interchangeable
A responder at a cardiac arrest wants ABCDE; at handover, ASHICE; for a scene report, ETHANE. Those are different jobs on different incidents, and the screen presents them as one continuous document. Nobody fills all four in.
"Send report" competes with Call 999
Two full-width pinned buttons, and the upper one is the signal colour. It is there from zero answers, occupying ~120px of the viewport and sitting above the one action that must never be missed.
"Record incident" is said twice
The navigation header and the body heading are identical, which spends a third of the first screen on nothing. Locate does not do this.
Progress is a grey sentence
"0 of 7 recorded" is the only progress signal, it is the least prominent text in the card, and it scrolls away the moment you start working.
The mnemonic's own structure is flattened
A-B-C-D-E is a sequence of five stages, and the screen renders it as one card of 21 fields. The structure that makes the mnemonic memorable is exactly what is thrown away.

A · One form at a time

The four become in-page tabs, the way Locate now works. One mnemonic's fields on screen; chips carry progress.

Gain: consistent with the navigation just built, and 8–21 fields instead of 47.

Cost: four long labels in one control is cramped on a 320px screen, and a chip row that scrolls hides the form you cannot see. It also assumes you know which mnemonic you want before you start.

Viable, but it is a switch, not a map

B · An index of four

Field is a menu. Each row names a mnemonic, what it describes, and how far along it is; opening one gives it a focused screen with its own header.

Gain: the screen is four lines instead of 47 fields, progress is the content, and a responder who needs only ASHICE taps once. It matches how the forms are actually used — one per incident.

Cost: one more tap to reach any form, and you cannot see two forms at once.

What I would ship

C · An accordion

All four stay on one screen, collapsed to a header each, expanding in place.

Gain: scope visible at a glance with no navigation at all.

Cost: expanding the longest form still pushes the rest off screen, so the benefit evaporates exactly where it is needed. Expanding state inside a scroll view is also fiddly to get right.

Looks tidy, fails at 21 fields

Three fixes that hold whichever structure wins

Send report leaves the pinned footer
It should not be a permanent primary above Call 999. Put it in the header with its count — "Send · 3 answered" — and leave the footer to the emergency action alone. The report is still always reachable, and the screen stops having two strongest buttons.
One title, not two
Drop the body heading and keep the navigation header, or the reverse. Two identical headings on one screen is the clearest sign the layout was never looked at on a phone.
Progress becomes a property of the form
The count belongs on the row or chip that names the form, so it is visible while you are deciding which one to open, and while you are inside it — not only when you happen to scroll past the card's header.
Not everything needs the same weight
Eighteen of ABCDE's fields are optional-by-nature. Some are equipment-dependent and say so; those could sit behind a "needs kit" disclosure so a first aider without a cuff or a thermometer is not scrolling past fields they cannot answer.

What I would ship

Option B, with the Send button moved into the header. The tab becomes a short menu of the four things the Responder depth actually provides, each with what it describes and how far along it is; opening one gives it the whole screen. ABCDE keeps A-B-C-D-E as stages, because that structure is the mnemonic.

The reason is not that four rows are prettier than 47 fields. It is that the screen should say which tool you are picking up, and the current one says "here is a very long form" — which is not true of any single incident.

I would not do Option C. It reads well as a sketch and collapses as soon as ABCDE is opened, which is the only case that matters.

What I need from you before building any of it
This is a design decision, so it needs your call rather than my implementation. The structure changes the routes (a focused form is a pushed screen), two Maestro flows (record-mnemonic-* selectors, which currently assume all four are on one page) and the Send path. If you want B, it is a plan-first change of its own — I would not fold it into the tab-navigation PR, which is already large and waiting on review.

How to read this. The frames above are 320 × 660 and drawn in the app's own palette and type. The field counts are counted from src/capture/forms.ts, not estimated, and the "today" frames follow the real screen, which was captured on the iOS simulator rather than remembered.
