Three directions, one decision

The three prototype files each show the same jobs — Act, CPR, the defibrillator list, where I am, responder capture, send — so they can be compared like for like. This page is the comparison: what each direction does with the same information architecture, and where each of the things you originally asked for ends up.

What is not being decided here: depth is not a mode, red is the emergency action and nothing else, offline is default, and clinical wording is reproduced and cited. All three respect those. The decision is narrower than it looks: how loud the app is, and whether the emergency action is a screen you return to or a piece of furniture.

Decided since this page was written

Beacon’s idea, on Workbench’s bottom tab navigation and sub-tabs. That includes the tab bar this page argued against, which is the owner’s call and a reasonable one — the tab bar is what gives the AED map and the capture forms a home.

The conflict it created is resolved in the merged prototype: the tab bar is always present, and the red Call 999 dock sits above it on every screen except Act, so there is exactly one red thing on any screen. The visual style is explicitly still open.

The recommendation below is kept as the reasoning, not as the decision.

What I’d ship

Beacon’s dock, Field Manual’s palette and restraint, Workbench’s Locate tab. That is a real recommendation, not a blend for its own sake: each direction got one thing right and one thing wrong, and they are different things.

Beacon is right that this app is used badly — one-handed, in poor light, by someone who may not be trained — and the dock is the honest fix for the fact that the current build keeps the most important action behind a back button. Field Manual is right that red and amber should stay scarce, and that type alone can carry a screen. Workbench is right that “walk to the defibrillator” should be one surface, not two taps and a screen.

The one thing I would not take is Workbench’s tab bar. Four equally weighted destinations at the bottom of the screen is exactly the “too many equal calls to action” the brief warns about, and it is the only idea here that makes the emergency path worse rather than better.

What these prototypes deliberately do not settle

  • The map is drawn in CSS. It exists so Direction 3’s composition can be judged; it is not a proposal for how MapLibre should render.
  • Settings, region packs, scan and about are nodes in the structure diagrams, not frames. They are built and their copy is unchanged by this decision, so they were left out to keep the rails comparable.
  • No motion. Transitions, the metronome’s beat and the dock’s behaviour under a scroll are all unspecified here.
  • Not run on a device. These were rendered in a headless browser and looked at; nothing has been through Maestro or a real phone.
